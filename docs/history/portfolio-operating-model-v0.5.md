# Portfolio automation and AI support: Ideal State

Version 0.5 | 12 September 2026 | Owner: Rex | Iteration 1 scenario decisions complete

**Status:** working synthesis with the first scenario-refinement iteration complete. The decision record captures Rex's confirmed direction. The central promise, criterion wording, checks, and audit method formalize that direction; unapproved design details remain proposals. No operational criterion is declared achieved. This document does not install automation or change existing execution, publishing, spending, or approval authority.

## Central promise

Rex runs his ventures as the sole human decision maker. He sets direction, chooses priorities, and decides what to build next. AI-supported operations turn that direction into useful, verified progress within agreed limits.

Each morning, one coherent cockpit lets him see the full portfolio: active initiatives, work in progress, unattended operations, progress toward each initiative's Ideal State, blocked progress, and the decisions that need him. Analysis explains what changed, why it matters, and what to do next. The system challenges his priorities when evidence supports a better choice.

Routine work and improvement loops continue without supervision. Ambiguous intent, major tradeoffs, and requests for scarce resources arrive as prepared recommendations. Decisions lead to action, checked results, and better-informed subsequent work.

OODA is the core decision loop: observe what happens, orient against intent and reality, decide within authority, and act with results feeding the next observation. The system supports Rex's judgment and delegates routine execution through clear intent and limits. Reports use plain business language.

Resources go first toward improving or protecting cash flow, then toward the greatest useful progress toward the Ideal State. Allocations are deliberate, and monthly reviews test whether the spending mattered. A one-hour morning queue remains an aspiration, not a current optimization target. Show the necessary work in full; adjust when unfinished reviews become a pattern or Rex asks to dial back.

A formal monthly Ideal State Review covers the initiatives: whether each destination remains right, what progress the evidence supports, and what should change. Evidence that undermines an Ideal State also triggers a challenge in the next morning's cockpit without waiting for the monthly review. Rex decides any change of direction.

Each initiative also develops an agreed operating model: the capabilities and recurring practices that improve the quality of its thinking and execution. Their maturity is visible alongside actual business results. Frameworks earn their place through useful decisions and dependable work.

The whole system runs as lean as possible while achieving these outcomes. Thrift applies to reasoning, execution, coordination, maintenance, and Rex's attention. Additional machinery and deeper analysis must earn their cost through a useful contribution.

## Scope and ownership

This Ideal State covers the combined working system across ventures, including shared services, project-specific flows, interactive AI work, scheduled jobs, event-driven workflows, external services, and the handoffs between them. A count of local scheduled jobs is not the full portfolio.

Each initiative retains its own authoritative Ideal State. Shared infrastructure has explicit outcomes and links to the initiatives it serves. One component owns each operational responsibility; several components may contribute to the same outcome. Missing capabilities belong in the assessment alongside existing automations.

**Proposed principle:** the cockpit presents those sources coherently rather than creating competing definitions of each initiative's direction. Personal memory, evidence, work queues, and implementation documents support the Ideal State without independently redefining it.

## Operating model quality and capability maturity

**Status:** proposed response to D15 and D16. This is a tailored approach for one human supported by AI, not adoption of an external certification scheme or a large-company organization chart.

There are three connected layers:

1. **Ideal State:** the outcomes and conditions the initiative seeks.
2. **Operating model:** the capabilities, responsibilities, and recurring practices intended to produce those outcomes.
3. **Capability maturity:** evidence of how well those practices are defined, used, dependable, and improving.

Business results and operating maturity remain separately visible. Good recent results can coexist with fragile operations. Disciplined operations can coexist with an unproven commercial proposition. A maturity score is evidence about operating capability, not a guarantee of future success.

### Proposed operating responsibilities

Derive coverage from the whole venture: choose its purpose and resources, define an offer, establish demand, fulfill the promise, provide the means of operating, and meet its obligations. These six responsibilities are the proposed partition. Thinking methods, quality attributes, and the feedback loop are separate dimensions rather than competing functional categories.

| Responsibility | Primary object it owns | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Direction and allocation | The initiative's purpose, Ideal State, business model, priorities, resource allocations, and start/scale/pause/close decisions. | Sets the mandate; does not own all routine actions within it. Rex retains the major decisions. |
| Offer and product development | What is offered: product/service scope, design, quality standards, roadmap, validation, and changes to the solution. | Defines and changes the promise; does not own winning demand or routinely fulfilling it. |
| Market and demand development | Market/customer evidence, positioning, acquisition, sales, channels, and commercial terms within authority. | Owns reaching and winning the right customers; fulfillment owns the experience after the commitment. Major pricing or business-model changes return to Rex. |
| Customer fulfillment and retention | Onboarding, routine production and service delivery, support, customer outcomes, renewals, and service recovery. | Owns delivery of the existing promise; new product capability belongs to offer development, and underlying shared infrastructure has its own owner. |
| Operating resources and infrastructure | Tools, agents, suppliers, capacity, coordination, technical infrastructure, and resource use within allocations. | Owns the means of work; consuming functions remain responsible for their use and business outcomes. |
| Business stewardship | Financial records, cash reconciliation, contracts, access policies, privacy/security obligations, and administrative requirements. | Owns accurate records and control requirements; functions execute the relevant controls in their own work. No new permission gate is implied. |

**Daily execution exists within every responsibility.** A recurring task is not automatically assigned to a general delivery bucket. Assign ownership by the primary output or decision that changes. The same small set of people, agents, and tools may cover several responsibilities; this is not a hiring or agent-role plan.

**OODA gives the performance and learning loop its structure.** Functional owners produce and use their evidence. The shared loop defines consistent measures, reconciles evidence, prepares the cockpit and reviews, preserves decisions, and follows their consequences. It owns those assessment outputs, not a second copy of every business outcome. Likewise, sound reasoning, quality, speed, reliability, and low owner effort are attributes assessed within the responsibilities where they matter.

#### Ownership and coverage checks

- Score the smallest useful responsibility or practice once. A workflow can cross functions; each handoff has a named output and accepting owner.
- A price change separates market evidence and the proposal from Rex's consequential decision, implementation of the offer, customer communication, and financial recording. These are connected outputs, not five owners of the same task.
- A production incident separates infrastructure restoration from customer support and protected-data obligations. A product correction and a review of the incident can follow without duplicating the original recovery result.
- A venture closure separates Rex's decision from customer wind-down, supplier termination, asset retirement, financial/other obligations, and preservation of lessons. Closure remains an owned journey.
- Shared services are assessed both as services with their own users and as dependencies of ventures. Their contribution is linked without counting downstream cash-flow gains twice.
- Unknown ownership, omitted work, and duplicate responsibility are audit findings. This proposed partition must pass the actual portfolio inventory; its labels alone do not prove exhaustive coverage.

### OODA at the core: the proposed civilian adaptation

The Marine Corps uses Boyd's OODA model to describe observation, orientation, decision, and action with feedback. MCDP 6 describes concurrent loops at different speeds, values meaningful action, and cautions that a neat sequence simplifies a more interactive process. Our application below is a proposal for this portfolio.

| Phase | What the system must do here | What prevents false progress |
|---|---|---|
| Observe | Gather dated operational, customer, economic, and external signals relevant to the initiative. | Missing or stale coverage remains visible. A worker's success claim is not independent outcome evidence. |
| Orient | Interpret signals against the current Ideal State, owner intent, prior decisions, constraints, and dependencies. Explain the consequence and plausible causes. | Distinguish observations, forecasts, assumptions, and inference. Look for competing explanations when they could change the decision. |
| Decide | Choose an authorized response, a bounded check, deliberate waiting, or a prepared escalation. | Respect allocations and reserved decisions. Rex interprets completed experiments and decides their next step, including passes and inconclusive results. |
| Act | Complete the authorized work and check what actually happened. Feed results, costs, and changed beliefs back into observation. | Completion of an action does not establish its expected benefit. No unbounded retries or automatic new authority. |

For consequential work, the intent is brief and stable: the outcome sought, why it matters, conditions to protect, available resources, and decisions reserved for Rex. An initiative can change its method within that authority when circumstances change. Changing Rex's purpose or constraints requires his decision. A fresh worker should be able to explain the intent and identify its source without inventing a shared understanding.

This is one operating loop expressed at several scales, not an additional scheduler:

- **Routine work:** respond when a relevant event or scheduled check warrants action, within existing authority and capacity.
- **Initiative improvement:** observe long enough to learn from the outcome. Do not repeatedly change a test before its result can be interpreted.
- **Daily portfolio review:** surface decisions, experiments, exceptions, and changed assessments for Rex; healthy work remains compact and inspectable.
- **Monthly allocation review:** compare spending and results, then return allocation choices to Rex.
- **Monthly Ideal State Review:** examine each initiative's direction, assumptions, and outcome evidence; prepare any changes for Rex. Reuse funding-review evidence.

Inspect the delay between a signal, its interpretation, a decision, an action, and a usable result. Explain whether delay comes from missing evidence, capacity, authorization, or a legitimate observation window. Shortening harmful delay is useful; counting loops or making every loop faster is not an outcome target. These checks extend existing logs and samples before any new measurement service is considered.

### Command discipline without a military organization

**Proposed adaptation:** borrow MAGTF's task organization: one accountable lead assembles existing capabilities and support around an outcome. Rex supplies intent and reserved decisions. This creates no permanent agent hierarchy or mapping of business functions to military units.

Use OSMEAC as an assignment checklist within the existing work record: context; current evidence; intended result and reason; approach and checks; resources and recovery; authority, coordination, and result location. Its orientation establishes context; OODA's Orient phase interprets evidence. Routine tasks reference standing instructions instead of generating fresh forms.

Upward reports use Minto: recommendation and decision first, followed by what changed, consequence, evidence and uncertainty, options, resources and displaced work, and next check. Assignment, funding, and result views reuse the same facts. Reporting adds no approval gate.

The analogy has limits: business success means customer value and trust; AI role names establish no competence; visibility must not create micromanagement. Check important claims against real evidence, preserve disagreement, and allow legitimate observation time. Fast loops can amplify noise or cost. Capability readiness remains separate from business results and adds no military score.

### Supporting thinking disciplines

**Proposed defaults:** Minto and MECE organize a scoped question, recommendation, and distinct evidence-backed reasons. Pareto directs attention to the few largest effects, using observed distributions rather than assuming 80/20. Sivers' *Anything You Want* informs small useful steps, owner-defined success, proportionate rules after failures, and responsibility while delegating.

Use The Great Mental Models selectively: first principles for assumptions, inversion for failure paths, second-order thinking for downstream effects, probabilities for uncertainty, and feedback models for recurring behavior. The public references support this selection; the full series has not been reviewed.

There is no model quota or adoption score. Use a method when it improves a consequential judgment or test. Stop analysis when the authorized next action and limits are clear; deepen it when uncertainty could change the choice. A neat argument does not establish truth. Simplicity changes no agreed standard, cash-flow priority, or experiment-review boundary.

A recurring practice records only the purpose, trigger, owner, inputs, changed work, and evidence needed to use it. Reuse existing records.

### Proposed scoring rubric

This is a custom scale, inspired by capability-maturity thinking. It is not a CMMI rating.

| Level | Meaning | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| 0: Absent | An applicable capability has no established practice. | Inspection supports absence. Lack of access or assessment is instead unknown. |
| 1: Defined | The practice is coherent, scoped, and assigned, but recurring use is unproven. | A usable definition identifies inputs, responsibilities, decisions, and outputs. |
| 2: Used | The practice demonstrably shapes real thinking or work. | Traceable examples show application and its effect on a decision or execution. |
| 3: Dependable | It works consistently under agreed conditions without Rex repeatedly reconnecting the steps. | Evidence across an agreed sample and period, including exceptions and owner effort. |
| 4: Improving | Feedback improves the practice's effectiveness or cost while it remains dependable. | Observed improvement over time, with outcome checks and retained counterevidence. |

Profiles show current level, stage-appropriate target, dated evidence and confidence, and the most valuable next improvement. Cover the six responsibilities plus OODA effectiveness, with project-specific practices where needed. Unknown differs from absent; not applicable needs a reason. Scores can fall. Critical gaps cannot disappear into an average or through excluding unknowns. No aggregate score, weighting, or pass threshold is approved.

Under D27, dependable practices that meet the initiative's needs count as success; further improvement must justify its cost. Maximum maturity is not required. AI titles, framework adoption, document volume, and automation volume earn no credit. Exploratory ventures can need lighter practices than operating services. Raising a score alone justifies neither resource use nor an approval gate; capability maturity does not prove customer benefit.

## Resource and decision rules

### Priority and time horizons

Cash-flow gains and avoided material losses are the first priority. Other Ideal State improvements are the second. Within these classes, the system explains expected magnitude, likelihood, timing, total resource needs, urgency, and the work that would wait. No numeric weighting formula has been agreed.

**Proposed reporting convention:** assign the earliest credible horizon for the first tangible intended result: by month 3, after month 3 through month 6, after month 6 through month 12, or beyond month 12. Record the date from which the horizon runs. Separate first evidence, first benefit, and recovery of investment; do not present an early milestone as a realized cash-flow result.

**Proposed economic convention:** show incremental cash inflows and outflows, timing, and assumptions. Revenue alone does not establish improved cash flow. Keep forecasts separate from observed results and avoid crediting the same benefit independently to several shared components.

**Confirmed time-value distinction (D22):** value Rex's time at EUR 75/hour for planning. Three hours released represent EUR 225 of nominal capacity, not EUR 225 earned. Keep forecast and observed cash, net owner hours, and their nominal value separate. Cash-generation work cannot be perpetually deferred in favor of theoretical time savings.

**Confirmed starting allocation rule (D23):** a time-saving case names the net hours released after setup and upkeep, what those hours could enable, and the evidence for that benefit. Favor removing an attention bottleneck when it enables valuable work; do not let nominal savings alone beat a credible cash opportunity. Review the realized use and outcome through existing monthly reviews without counting both nominal capacity and its eventual cash result as separate cash gains. A fixed cash-work allocation has not been agreed.

A funding case identifies the outcome and criterion served, expected benefit, agreed test, resources requested, constraints, alternatives, time horizon, and stopping conditions. The monthly review compares results with that original case and recommends the next allocation. A longer-horizon initiative is assessed against meaningful interim evidence while its final outcome remains pending.

Money, tokens, execution capacity, elapsed time, and human attention are distinct constraints. Token counts alone are not total cost. Approved, committed, consumed, and remaining resources should be distinguishable. Numerical budgets, concurrency limits, and accounting conventions are still open decisions.

### Major decisions that come to Rex

The accepted starting categories are:

1. Start, pause, or close an initiative; change its audience, business model, or Ideal State direction.
2. Move portfolio resources in a way that delays or displaces agreed work.
3. Exceed an allocation, create a new ongoing obligation, or fund a strategic bet beyond 12 months.
4. Materially change pricing, guarantees, service promises, or customer-data use.
5. Expand an automation's authority or remove an approval requirement.
6. Sacrifice one agreed outcome for another, such as weakening a quality standard to increase revenue.
7. Take a hard-to-reverse action that risks material loss, damaged trust, or destruction without a proven recovery path.

The target is to establish usable standing boundaries and reuse them. It is not to request the same permission repeatedly. An action within an existing approval remains authorized; a routine label does not independently grant permission.

When a rule blocks useful work, the system identifies the actual rule, the blocked outcome, and a concrete proposed change. It continues independent authorized work. It does not silently stall or bypass the boundary.

### Failure allowance and experiments

The confirmed failure allowance is specific: a nonessential public page may be broken for up to one hour during an automated improvement if payments, customer data, and core service remain unaffected and recovery is available.

**Proposed operational interpretation:** measure the hour from actual service impairment, not eventual detection. Restore or roll back within that hour. Define which pages are nonessential and demonstrate recovery before this becomes an unattended authority. Repeated disruptions must remain visible; a new attempt must not silently reset an unresolved incident's clock. No frequency allowance has been agreed.

Other failure allowances must state the affected scope, maximum duration or loss, recovery method, and escalation boundary. An approved experiment may fail its hypothesis within its allocation; that does not excuse exceeding limits or harming unrelated services.

**Proposed execution rule:** an experiment stops at its agreed completion, spending, or safety limit while its results await Rex. The result packet includes the original hypothesis and pass/fail criteria, observed results, uncertainty, resource use, and a recommendation. Rex interprets the result and decides the next step. Other funded work continues independently.

**Confirmed distinction (D21):** restoring agreed behavior is routine repair and verification. Changing a working form to test a conversion hypothesis is an experiment. Calling that change routine optimization does not remove Rex's result-review requirement.

## Ideal State criteria and checks

All criterion formulations below are **proposed**. Their decision basis is recorded explicitly. All current attainment statuses are **not assessed**: this discovery defines the target and does not establish live operation. Each eventual check must record its date, source, sample, observation window, and limitations. The audit may split compound criteria into smaller independently testable claims without changing the owner outcome.

### AUTO-IS-01: Rex sees the complete operating portfolio

**Desired state:** The morning cockpit accounts for every in-scope initiative, its active work, unattended responsibilities, and unresolved handoffs. Shared dependencies and missing coverage are visible.

**Decision basis:** D01, D02.

**Check:** Reconcile the cockpit against an independently assembled initiative and operating inventory, including remote services and interactive work. Trace sampled work across component boundaries.

**Counterevidence:** A necessary flow is absent, abandoned work appears active, or successful components conceal a broken handoff.

**Next gap:** Establish the scope inventory and responsibility map; do not assume historical scheduler documentation is complete or current.

### AUTO-IS-02: Progress means observed movement toward the right outcome

**Desired state:** Each initiative's assessment references its current Ideal State, shows dated evidence, and distinguishes results from implementation or activity.

**Decision basis:** D02, D05; existing project Ideal State framework.

**Check:** Trace sampled progress claims to the applicable criterion and supporting result. Inspect a missed outcome despite successful job runs, and an outcome with no measurement yet.

**Counterevidence:** Run counts, shipped features, or generated documents establish success by themselves; unknown results display as failures or successes.

**Next gap:** Identify current authoritative artifacts, their approval status, and available outcome evidence for each initiative.

### AUTO-IS-03: Rex receives prepared, consequential decisions

**Desired state:** Each decision explains the problem, why it matters, options, recommendation, evidence, tradeoffs, resource needs, and consequence of waiting. It challenges existing priorities when warranted.

**Decision basis:** D02, D03, D14, D24.

**Check:** Review real decision packets and record the extra reconstruction Rex needs. Include a case where evidence argues against a current priority.

**Direction challenge:** When completed customer tests credibly undermine the business case despite sound execution, the next cockpit identifies the failed assumption, evidence strength, options, and recommended change. It does not wait for the monthly review or implement a strategic change without Rex.

**Counterevidence:** Rex must inspect logs to understand the issue, receives agreeable summaries without analysis, or sees a new priority silently substituted for his own.

**Next gap:** Agree a compact decision format and collect a baseline of clarification effort.

### AUTO-IS-04: Clear authorized improvements complete without supervision

**Desired state:** The system consults the initiative's Ideal State, diagnoses gaps, executes suitable remedies within constraints, and checks results without repeated permission requests.

**Decision basis:** D03, D08, D09, D21.

**Check:** Trace a routine improvement through diagnosis, action, and outcome verification, including an absent-owner period and a recoverable failure.

**Boundary check:** A broken signup form can be restored and verified within standing authority without creating an experiment-result approval request.

**Counterevidence:** Work waits for redundant approval, runs without understanding the goal, or is marked complete after only an attempted action.

**Next gap:** Map actual authority and recovery boundaries for sampled flows.

### AUTO-IS-05: Major decisions and exhausted limits remain under Rex's control

**Desired state:** Major decisions arrive prepared; actions stay within their authority and resource limits while unrelated authorized work continues.

**Decision basis:** D03, D08, D09, D11.

**Check:** Exercise an in-budget major tradeoff, a likely overrun, missing owner response, and a routine action already covered by approval. Check both required stops and unnecessary stops.

**Counterevidence:** Silence grants permission, a budget permits a strategic change, or one blocked decision freezes unrelated work.

**Next gap:** Turn the accepted categories into concrete flow-specific examples without adding blanket approval gates.

### AUTO-IS-06: Portfolio capacity goes to the most valuable justified work

**Desired state:** Work is scoped before commitment and selected against shared resource limits, with cash-flow improvement or protection first, then greater Ideal State progress.

**Decision basis:** D04, D05, D06, D07, D20, D22, D23. The aspirational morning hour is not an allocation limit; the time-value assumption is not cash income.

**Check:** Compare simultaneous funding requests, their assumptions, resource reservations, chosen order, and displaced work. Include a material-loss prevention case and a speculative growth case.

**Time-value check:** A case releasing three owner hours shows EUR 225 nominal value and separately reports any evidenced cash effect. Inspect whether repeated time-saving work has displaced the first credible cash-generating action.

**Counterevidence:** Each initiative assumes the full shared capacity, forecasts omit added cost, or work wins because it is easy to run rather than useful.

### AUTO-IS-07: Monthly funding decisions learn from results

**Desired state:** A monthly review compares each allocation with meaningful results and informs continuation, expansion, reduction, redirection, or stopping.

**Decision basis:** D06, D07, D23.

**Check:** Follow a funding case from original expectations to observed spend, outcomes, recommendation, and Rex's allocation decision. Test a benefit that is not yet due.

**Time-saving case:** Compare promised net hours with hours actually released, their use, and resulting benefits. Unused capacity is not recorded as realized cash income.

**Counterevidence:** Funding renews by habit, spending is justified by activity, or a twelve-month outcome is declared failed solely because it has not arrived in month one.

**Next gap:** Choose the review date and establish the first funding register and baseline.

### AUTO-IS-08: Experiment outcomes return to Rex before the next experiment decision

**Desired state:** Both passes and failures reach the next morning's cockpit with the original criteria, evidence, uncertainty, costs, and recommended next step. Rex decides what follows.

**Decision basis:** D11, D12, D21.

**Check:** Trace one pass, one failure, and one inconclusive outcome. Verify that stopping at a limit does not silently become a strategic abandonment or approval to repeat.

**Boundary check:** Testing fewer fields on a working signup form retains the hypothesis, criteria, and Rex's result decision, even when labeled routine optimization.

**Counterevidence:** A passing result automatically scales spending, or a failed experiment is closed as a strategic decision without Rex's review.

**Next gap:** Identify which current flows are experiments and where their predeclared criteria and review decisions live.

### AUTO-IS-09: Attention arrives at the right time

**Desired state:** Routine decisions wait for the morning cockpit; matters where waiting could cause material loss receive timely alerts with prepared next actions.

**Decision basis:** D02, D12.

**Check:** Sample routine and urgent events, evidence of delivery, duplicates, and unresolved decisions. Include an alert-delivery failure.

**Counterevidence:** Repeated low-value alerts consume attention, an urgent threat waits overnight, or an accepted send is treated as proof Rex received or acted on it.

**Next gap:** Define materiality, contact channels, expected response assumptions, and fallback behavior within existing authority.

### AUTO-IS-10: Necessary decision work stays visible while attention load is learned

**Desired state:** The cockpit displays all necessary decision work. One hour remains the Ideal State aspiration, but initial operation does not optimize the queue to that number. A pattern of unfinished reviews or Rex's request triggers a workload adjustment within existing authority; consequential reallocations remain his decisions.

**Decision basis:** D13, clarified by D20.

**Check:** In the 50-plus-30-minute scenario, show all 80 minutes of necessary work without recommending delay merely to fit the hour. Track actual review time, context gathering, follow-ups, unfinished decisions and age, and attention outside the morning. Check the response to recurring unfinished reviews and to Rex asking to dial back.

**Counterevidence:** Work is hidden or delayed solely to hit 60 minutes; effort is shifted outside the measured queue; recurring unfinished work is ignored; or Rex's request to reduce load has no effect.

**Next gap:** Establish a lightweight baseline of actual attention load and completion. No fixed count of unfinished reviews is agreed; elapsed review time alone is not failure.

### AUTO-IS-11: Accepted failures stay bounded and recoverable

**Desired state:** Automation can move quickly within explicit failure allowances. It detects and recovers from failures before the accepted limits are exceeded.

**Decision basis:** D09, D10, D26.

**Check:** Demonstrate the accepted nonessential-page case in a safe drill, including detection, recovery time, and unaffected core services. Inspect repeat incidents and the actual recovery path.

**Interruption check:** Stop a worker after an action succeeds but before it records completion. Its replacement recovers the authority and intent, checks the actual outcome, and resumes without replaying the completed action. If evidence remains insufficient, it holds that action and surfaces consequential uncertainty while independent work continues.

**Counterevidence:** Recovery requires Rex during the tolerated window, the incident exceeds an hour, protected services are affected, or the same allowance is assumed for unrelated failures.

**Next gap:** Name eligible pages and verify recovery. Resolve other failure tolerances individually as flows are assessed.

### AUTO-IS-12: OODA connects evidence, judgment, authorized action, and learning

**Desired state:** Each initiative observes relevant signals, interprets them against current intent, decides within authority, and acts with checked results feeding subsequent work. A fresh worker receives the current direction, decisions, and evidence without rewriting Rex's goals.

**Decision basis:** D02, D03, D14, D17, D26; existing shared learning and Ideal State guidance. The detailed implementation remains a proposal.

**Check:** Follow a real signal through interpretation, decision authority, action, checked outcome, and the next worker's context. Include a contradictory result, unavailable source, routine authorized response, and experiment awaiting Rex. Explain observed delay and resource use.

**Counterevidence:** Rex repeats an accepted decision, an AI proposal becomes owner policy, stored knowledge never affects later work, or a fast sequence of jobs is presented as a completed learning loop without outcome evidence.

**Next gap:** Trace the current capture, retrieval, and evidence-update paths across the actual clients and runners.

### AUTO-IS-13: Operating rules enable useful work without hidden stalls

**Desired state:** Every enforced boundary has a clear purpose. When it obstructs authorized progress, the system exposes the conflict and proposes a specific resolution while continuing independent work.

**Decision basis:** D03, D09.

**Check:** Examine blocked work and repeated approval requests. Pair each important rule with a case it should stop and a legitimate case it should allow.

**Counterevidence:** Work is silently abandoned, vague risk invokes new gates, or removing friction means bypassing an actual owner boundary.

**Next gap:** Audit the real instructions, approval paths, and handoffs for conflicting or redundant rules.

### AUTO-IS-14: Agreed practices improve how each initiative thinks and executes

**Desired state:** Each initiative has an operating model suited to its stage and outcomes. Its capability profile reflects actual use, dependable execution, and learning. Frameworks improve decisions without creating unnecessary work or approval barriers.

**Decision basis:** D15, D16, D27, constrained by D04, D05, D09, and D13. The responsibility categories, selected practices, and scoring rubric above remain proposed.

**Check:** Trace a sampled practice from evidence through judgment to changed work and observed consequences. Inspect its owner effort and maintenance cost. Compare the claimed maturity with repeated real use, including a counterexample and an unknown area.

**Adequacy check:** Retain a dependable process when a proposed extra review layer offers only a higher maturity score. Further improvement identifies a useful expected outcome and justifies setup and recurring costs; existing agreed checks continue.

**Counterevidence:** Scores rise through document creation alone; a critical gap is hidden by an average; a practice adds work without improving judgment or results; or a mature process is treated as proof of a viable initiative.

**Next gap:** Select the core frameworks with Rex, tailor targets to initiative stages, and calibrate the rubric on real examples before calculating a portfolio score.

### AUTO-IS-15: The system earns the cost of its thinking and machinery

**Desired state:** The portfolio achieves its agreed outcomes with the least justified total effort and complexity, including reasoning, execution, coordination, maintenance, and owner attention.

**Decision basis:** D18, D19, D27, constrained by existing outcome and authority decisions.

**Clarified boundary:** A healthy initiative receives the short check. Deeper analysis identifies the unresolved question or agreed discovery budget that justifies it. This preserves required checks and existing authority.

**Check:** Compare a sampled flow with a credible simpler alternative across outcomes, token use, elapsed time, repair work, and owner effort. Identify what additional analysis or machinery contributes. Sample the combined system as well as individual flows.

**Scenario check:** Fresh evidence and met targets permit the short check without a full review. Missing evidence, a missed target, or a new issue requires the appropriate bounded investigation or escalation; it cannot pass as healthy. Discretionary deep reviews without a question or discovery allocation contradict D19.

**Counterevidence:** Extra reviews change no decision; duplicated reports require reconciliation; a cheaper run creates greater repair costs; or simplification drops required coverage or approvals.

**Next gap:** Calibrate the short check and investigation triggers on real work. No universal agent count or session time limit is established.

### AUTO-IS-16: Monthly Ideal State Reviews test the direction across initiatives

**Desired state:** Each month, Rex receives and conducts a formal review across initiatives of whether their Ideal States remain justified, what progress is evidenced, and which changes need his decision.

**Decision basis:** D25, supported by D02, D14, D24; D07 and D18 support reusing spend-review preparation.

**Proposed lean format:** one portfolio review record, linked to each authoritative Ideal State and its evidence. For each initiative, show current direction, progress and gaps, challenged assumptions, recommendation, and Rex's decision or unresolved question. Combine with the monthly funding session where practical. Valid direction can be reaffirmed; the review does not require a rewrite.

**Check:** Reconcile the monthly record with the initiative inventory. Trace a retained direction, a challenged assumption, and any approved revision to its source and subsequent work. Unknown evidence stays visible; delayed outcomes are judged against their agreed horizons.

**Counterevidence:** The review only lists completed tasks or spending; an initiative is omitted; failed delivery is concealed by weakening its Ideal State; or a proposal becomes owner direction without his decision.

**Next gap:** Choose the monthly date and calibrate this compact format. Calendar scheduling and live review automation are not established by this document.

## How the later audit should use this artifact

The following method is proposed, not a completed assessment or an implementation queue.

For every initiative and flow, identify its intended outcome, primary criterion, consumers, actual entrypoint, authority, dependencies, resources, and observed result. Examine missing capabilities and duplicate responsibilities as well as existing machinery. Test whole journeys across shared and project-specific components.

Keep three judgments separate:

1. **Purpose:** Is the idea worth pursuing, and does the flow address the right outcome?
2. **Execution:** Does the complete flow actually deliver reliably within its authority and limits?
3. **Net contribution:** Do its benefits justify its total resource demand and complexity compared with available alternatives?

These distinctions allow findings such as worthwhile and effective; worthwhile but broken; well executed but low value; harmful in purpose and execution; missing; or unproven. Link each judgment to dated evidence and confidence. Avoid an overall percentage complete that hides critical gaps or treats unknowns as failures.

Recommend repair, simplify, combine, retire, add a missing capability, or gather evidence only after stating the desired outcome and current gap. Record the expected cash-flow or Ideal State benefit, horizon, resources, authority needed, test, and stopping rule. Recommendations are not automatic permission to change live systems.

## Evidence and revision rules

- Separate owner decisions, agent proposals, user reports, implementation evidence, and observed outcomes.
- Use explicit attainment states: not assessed, not measured, partial, supported within stated scope, or contradicted. Record scope, date, source, and limits.
- Missing evidence remains unknown. Repeated AI summaries do not count as independent corroboration.
- Preserve failed checks, conflicting observations, and superseded decisions with their sources.
- Reopen supported claims when evidence becomes stale or contradictory. Do not weaken a criterion to make work look successful.
- Update evidence as work produces results. Record Rex's explicit decisions without asking him to approve them again. Inferred strategic changes remain proposals.
- Keep this artifact's revision history and supporting evidence distinct. Its eventual canonical home is still to be chosen; this file is the local working draft.
